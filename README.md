## Overview

This repository contains teaching and learning resources developed for the undergraduate course **BT501 – Foundations of Blockchain Technology**.

The repository is designed to help students understand the theoretical foundations, cryptographic principles, architecture, consensus mechanisms, and practical implementation of blockchain technology.

It brings together classroom notes, presentations, pre-reading materials, laboratory exercises, question banks, and Solidity-based practical work in one place.

The course follows a concept-to-application approach, connecting fundamental blockchain principles with real-world blockchain platforms and smart contract development.

## Course Learning Objectives

By studying the materials in this repository, students should be able to:

- Understand blockchain architecture and the fundamentals of distributed ledger technology.
- Explain how blocks, transactions, and cryptographic hash functions work.
- Understand public-key cryptography and digital signatures.
- Explain Merkle trees and their role in blockchain data verification.
- Compare consensus mechanisms such as Proof of Work (PoW) and Proof of Stake (PoS).
- Understand Ethereum architecture and the Ethereum Virtual Machine (EVM).
- Develop and understand basic smart contracts using Solidity.
- Explore blockchain governance, applications, and emerging use cases.

## Practical Learning – Solidity and Smart Contracts

The practical component introduces students to the development and execution of smart contracts.

Students can use the Solidity laboratory resources to understand:

- Solidity syntax and contract structure.
- State variables, functions, and data types.
- Visibility and access modifiers.
- Contract compilation and deployment.
- Reading and modifying smart contract state.
- Transactions, gas, and block execution.
- Basic smart contract security considerations.

The practical workflow involves writing a smart contract, compiling it, deploying it to a development environment, and interacting with its functions.

## Learning Approach

The course encourages students to follow a structured learning process:

1. **Pre-read:** Study the assigned material before the classroom session.
2. **Understand:** Develop conceptual clarity through lectures, diagrams, and examples.
3. **Practice:** Solve questions and work through cryptographic and blockchain examples.
4. **Implement:** Write and execute Solidity smart contracts.
5. **Analyse:** Observe transaction execution, gas consumption, and contract behaviour.
6. **Revise:** Use unit-wise summary notes and the question bank for revision.
